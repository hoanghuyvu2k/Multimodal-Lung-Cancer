\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our original research article entitled
\textbf{``NLP-Augmented Multimodal Attention Fusion for Immunotherapy
Response Prediction in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer''} for consideration for
publication in \emph{Cancers}. The manuscript comprises approximately 4,600
words of main text (Introduction through Conclusions; abstract 192 words),
5 main figures, 8 main tables, and a supplementary file containing 3
supplementary notes, 5 supplementary figures, and 3 supplementary tables.
The authorship list is: [First Author]$^{1,\dagger}$, [Second Author]$^{2}$,
and [Third Author]$^{1,*}$ (corresponding author).

Predicting which patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
will respond to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy remains an
unresolved clinical problem, as established biomarkers such as PD-L1
expression provide only modest discrimination. In this study, we extend a
previously developed multimodal attention fusion model (DyAM), which
combines CT radiomics, pathology imaging features, tumour genomics, PD-L1
status, and clinical data, with two contributions: (1) a competitive
``one-vs-others'' (OvO) attention mechanism, evaluated under a repeated-seed
protocol against the original cooperative attention, and (2) a
natural-language-processing-based encoding of structured clinical variables
using a pretrained sentence transformer, in place of conventional numeric
encoding. Using a discovery cohort of 247 patients with advanced NSCLC
treated with ICIs, we show that OvO attention matches the original
cooperative attention at every primary benchmark configuration (within
0.011~AUC, $p \geq 0.17$; AUC~$=0.773$ vs.\ $0.760$ at the primary
benchmark), offering a simpler parameterization without a measurable
accuracy cost, and that NLP-based clinical encoding
consistently improves both response-prediction AUC (up to
$\Delta\text{AUC}=+0.030$) and survival risk stratification (highest Cox
hazard ratios and time-dependent AUC among all tested clinical encodings,
all Kaplan--Meier log-rank $p<0.005$); this improvement generalises across
both attention mechanisms, indicating the encoding strategy itself, not the
attention architecture, drives the gain.

We believe this work is well-suited to \emph{Cancers} given the journal's
strong focus on translational oncology, multimodal biomarker discovery, and
applications of artificial intelligence to cancer diagnosis and treatment
selection. Our findings offer a practical, fine-tuning-free strategy
(NLP encoding of structured EHR data) that is readily transferable to other
multimodal oncology prediction tasks, and our repeated-seed comparison of
attention mechanisms illustrates the importance of robustness testing beyond
a single cross-validation partition when evaluating multimodal fusion
architectures.

previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere, in

and we are happy to suggest reviewers if required.

Thank you for considering our manuscript. We look forward to your response.
