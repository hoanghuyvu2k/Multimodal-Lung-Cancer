\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We extended the DyAM multimodal attention framework for ICI response prediction in NSCLC with a competitive (OvO) attention mechanism and an NLP-based
encoding of structured clinical variables, and the repeated cross-validation (5~seeds~$\times$~10-fold) over 21 modality combinations supports four conclusions.
First, fusing sources is what raises performance: four fused sources reached a mean AUC of $0.763$ and, with the NLP encoding, $0.786 \pm 0.017$, against $0.720$
for PD-L1~TPS alone, and the association of the risk score with progression-free survival grew with the number of fused sources.
Second, the NLP encoding of clinical variables with a general-purpose sentence transformer exceeded numeric labs in all six comparisons
($+0.017$ and $+0.020$ AUC in the two pathology arms, $+0.014$ to $+0.024$ in the two seven-source configurations), without any fine-tuning on clinical text.
Third, OvO attention, with about a quarter of the trainable parameters, had the higher mean AUC at the primary benchmark ($0.771$ against $0.763$) and in most configurations
fusing five or more sources, and it had a higher log hazard ratio, 12-month time-dependent AUC and C-index than cooperative attention in all eight configurations with
five to seven sources; with the NLP encoding it gave the highest log hazard ratio ($0.495$ against $0.416$, higher in all five seeds).
Fourth, the approach is practical, since encoding a patient takes less than one second on CPU and needs no labelled clinical text.
An order-based hold-out evaluation gave lower estimates ($0.703$ for OvO+NLP and $0.700$ for DyAM+NLP), so cross-validated AUCs should not be read as expected performance
in new patients.
Validation in larger, multi-institutional cohorts with calendar dates is needed to confirm these findings and to establish their clinical utility for treatment selection.
